# GFPA-250: Access Point and Entry Control Management

**Issuing body (fictional):** Global Facility Protection Alliance (GFPA)
**Edition:** 1st Edition (Demonstration) — Fictional publication, not in force anywhere

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This standard defines minimum management requirements for facility entry points, including public entrances, staff and vendor access points, and emergency-only exits, in proportion to their number and function.

## 2. Entry Point Inventory and Classification

2.1. Every facility shall maintain a current inventory of all entry points, classified as: primary public entrance, secondary/staff entrance, vendor or loading access, and emergency-only egress.

2.2. Each entry point shall be assigned an access control method appropriate to its classification: staffed monitoring, electronic access control, mechanical locking, or a combination thereof.

## 3. Requirements Scaled to Entry Point Count

3.1. Facilities with a higher total count of entry points present a proportionally larger control surface and shall apply additional compensating measures as the count increases, including: increased surveillance coverage at each point (GFPA-201), documented periodic testing of access control mechanisms, and inclusion of all entry points in scheduled safety inspections (GFPA-240).

3.2. Emergency-only egress points shall be fitted with alarm-only hardware that permits immediate exit but logs or signals any activation, and shall never be secured in a manner that impedes emergency egress, regardless of facility classification.

3.3. Vendor and loading access points shall not be left in an unmonitored, unlocked state outside of active, supervised use.

## 4. Periodic Testing

4.1. Electronic access control systems shall be functionally tested no less than quarterly.

4.2. Mechanical locking hardware shall be inspected for wear or tampering at each scheduled safety inspection (GFPA-240).

## 5. Relationship to Other Controls

5.1. This standard recognizes that entry point count and access control quality interact with, but do not substitute for, the surveillance coverage (GFPA-201) and staffing (GFPA-210) requirements defined elsewhere in this framework — a well-controlled entry point inventory does not by itself offset deficiencies in either area.
