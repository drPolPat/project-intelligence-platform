# GFPA-230: Incident and Near-Miss Reporting, Classification, and Escalation Procedures

**Issuing body (fictional):** Global Facility Protection Alliance (GFPA)
**Edition:** 3rd Edition (Demonstration) — Fictional publication, not in force anywhere

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This standard defines minimum requirements for reporting, classifying, and escalating security and safety incidents, including near-misses, at facilities operating under this framework.

## 2. Severity Classification

2.1. **Near-Miss:** an event with no realized injury or loss, logged for trend-monitoring purposes.

2.2. **Minor:** an event addressed on-site with no further escalation required.

2.3. **Moderate:** an event requiring facility management notification and documented corrective follow-up.

2.4. **Major:** an event requiring escalation to facility leadership and a formal incident review.

2.5. **Critical:** an event requiring activation of emergency response procedures and a full incident review and external reporting where applicable.

## 3. Reporting Timelines

3.1. Near-Miss and Minor events shall be logged within 5 business days of occurrence.

3.2. Moderate events shall be reported to facility management within 24 hours.

3.3. Major and Critical events shall be reported to facility leadership immediately upon discovery, and in no case later than 4 hours after the event is identified.

## 4. Escalation Path

4.1. Any event initially classified below Major that is later determined to involve a Tier 1 structural vulnerability (GFPA-215) or a pattern of 3 or more related events within a rolling 90-day period shall be re-classified upward by at least one severity level.

4.2. Facilities shall maintain a documented escalation contact list, reviewed at each scheduled safety inspection (GFPA-240).

## 5. Recordkeeping

5.1. All incident and near-miss records shall be retained for a minimum of 24 months and made available for review during scheduled safety inspections and risk assessments.

5.2. Aggregate incident trends, including category and severity distribution, should inform facility staffing (GFPA-210) and surveillance coverage (GFPA-201) planning on an ongoing basis rather than only at the time of a formal risk assessment.
